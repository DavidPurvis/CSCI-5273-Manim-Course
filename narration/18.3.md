# Dish 18.3: Say it out loud

## 18.3.open
Recite. Say each answer out loud before I do. First: the BGP selection order, in the lecture's simplified version.

## 18.3.p1
Go ahead.

## 18.3.a1
Highest local preference. Then shortest AS path. Then lowest internal cost to the exit. Then a tie break. Compare in order, and stop as soon as one route remains.

## 18.3.p2
Next: the commercial export rule. Who hears a route learned from a customer? Who hears a route learned from a peer or a provider?

## 18.3.a2
Own and customer routes go to everyone. Peer and provider routes go only to customers. Would this make me carry traffic between two non-customers? Then don't advertise it. That gives valley-free paths: up, at most one peer link, then down.

## 18.3.p3
Next: multicast defaults. What does dense mode do by default? And sparse mode?

## 18.3.a3
Dense mode forwards unless pruned. Sparse mode sends nothing until joined, starting with a shared star comma G tree through the RP, and it may switch to S comma G. IGMP learns local interest, and PIM connects the routers.

## 18.3.p4
Last: the handover stages. What are the data paths before, during, and after?

## 18.3.a4
Before: S-GW, A, phone. During: S-GW, A, B, phone, through temporary forwarding. After: S-GW, B, phone. Prepare, move the radio, forward, switch the core path, release.
