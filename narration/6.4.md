# Dish 6.4: VLANs and trunks

## 6.4.question
A building has one set of switches, but two departments that should not share broadcasts. Finance sits on three different floors, mixed in with engineering. Do we have to rewire the building each time someone changes desks?

## 6.4.vlans
A virtual LAN lets one physical switching system act as several separate LANs. Group by organization rather than by location. A broadcast from a finance machine is now flooded only to finance ports, and engineering never sees it. A flood is confined to its VLAN, even though every frame crosses the same box.

## 6.4.membership
How does a switch know which VLAN a frame belongs to? There are two ways. With port-based VLANs, membership belongs to the port. Ports 1 through 8 are VLAN 10, and ports 9 through 16 are VLAN 20. Whatever plugs into that port joins that VLAN. With MAC-based VLANs, membership follows the machine's address instead, so a laptop keeps its VLAN wherever it plugs in.

## 6.4.vlan-trace-1
An independent-learning example. Ports 1 and 2 are VLAN 10. Ports 3 and 4 are VLAN 20. Port 5 is a trunk for both. Port 6 is in VLAN 10 but discards data. A frame from A enters port 1 for unknown B. Learn A in VLAN 10 on port 1. Flood only ports 2 and 5.

## 6.4.vlan-trace-2
The same MAC, A, enters port 3 in VLAN 20. Learn a separate entry for A in VLAN 20 on port 3. B is unknown in this VLAN too, so flood only ports 4 and 5. The VLAN 10 entry is still there. This example uses independent learning, rather than claiming all hardware organizes its memory identically.

## 6.4.vlan-trace-3
B replies on port 2 in VLAN 10. Learn B in VLAN 10 on port 2, then look up A within VLAN 10. The frame goes only to port 1. Neither the other VLAN nor the discarding port receives it.

## 6.4.vlan-trace-4
Finally B replies on port 4 in VLAN 20. Learn B in VLAN 20 on port 4, then look up A within that VLAN. Send only to port 3. The VLAN context chooses which learned location is relevant. The trunk can carry both groups while keeping their identities separate.

## 6.4.trunk
Finance spans two switches, joined by one cable. That cable carries both departments. When a frame arrives at the far switch, how can it tell which VLAN the frame belongs to? The sending switch inserts a tag. This is 802.1Q trunking. It adds four bytes after the source address, including a 12 bit VLAN ID. Because the frame changed, the CRC is recomputed. The receiving switch reads the tag, and removes it before delivering to an ordinary host.

## 6.4.devices
Before leaving the LAN, compare the three boxes we now have. A hub gives you no traffic isolation, and it needs no setup. A switch isolates traffic, and it's still plug and play, but flooding and spanning trees keep it from routing efficiently. An IP router isolates traffic and routes well, but it isn't plug and play, because someone must configure it. Each step up costs something, and buys something.

## 6.4.rule
Keep the jobs separate. A VLAN decides who shares a broadcast domain. A trunk carries that identity between switches. Neither one moves traffic between different VLANs. For that, you need a router, even if the router lives inside a multilayer switch.

## 6.4.pitfall
The slide's phrase can mislead you here. A trunk carries frames between switches, keeping their VLANs separate. It doesn't move traffic from one VLAN to another. That takes a router.

## 6.4.changed
Change one thing. A finance laptop's owner moves desks, and plugs into a port in VLAN 20, the engineering VLAN, on a port-based switch.

## 6.4.predict
Whose broadcasts does the laptop hear now? And can it keep its old IP address and still work?

## 6.4.answer
It now hears engineering's broadcasts, because the port decided its membership. And if each VLAN is its own IP subnet, its old address no longer fits, so it needs a new configuration. The laptop is physically fine, yet logically it moved. Remember too that VLANs separate traffic, but they are no complete security proof. Addresses can be changed, and configuration decides what is accepted. That word, subnet, needs a real meaning. And it is the point where local delivery runs out. To go further, we need addresses with structure.
