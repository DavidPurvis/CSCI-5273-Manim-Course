# Dish 18.4: The slide clarifications

## 18.4.open
Finally, the places where the slides need a careful reading. These are the corrections and scope notes we met along the way. No new material. For each one, keep the corrected version in mind.

## 18.4.lan
On LANs. The slide's table TTL is a local aging timer in the switch, not a hop count in the frame. And spanning tree, shortest-path trees, and minimum spanning trees have different objectives.

## 18.4.ip
On IP. The maximum IPv4 total length is 65,535 bytes, not the 63,535 printed on the slide, because the field has 16 bits. In the subnet example, 128.101.5.0/23 normalizes to 128.101.4.0/23, and the 31-host department needs a slash 26. And the 51.2 nanosecond budget leaves out Ethernet's physical overhead.

## 18.4.dns
On discovery. DNS has 13 root identities, A through M, and each is deployed as many physical instances. And the slide's quick TCP recap leaves out the final ACK of the handshake.

## 18.4.mc
On anycast and multicast. Anycast's problem with TCP is lost connection state, not a blanket incompatibility. IGMP queries go to all hosts, while a report goes to the group being reported. The slide's membership timer computes 270 seconds, while the RFC's default formula gives 260. And sparse mode begins with a shared tree through the RP, whatever the slide's shorthand says.

## 18.4.radio
On wireless and mobility. Four-address Wi-Fi is a bridging case, not an ad hoc rule. The exposed-terminal shortcut is idealized. IMSI is a decimal subscriber identity of at most 15 digits, not a MAC address. And the foreign-agent drawing is one Mobile IPv4 arrangement, separate from LTE.
