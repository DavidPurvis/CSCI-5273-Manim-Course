# Dish 17.4: Practice question 4: fragmentation

## 17.4.question
Practice question 4. A 2,600 byte IPv4 datagram, with a 20 byte header and no options, may be fragmented over an MTU of 1,000. Give each fragment's payload length, total length, offset, and more fragments flag. Then explain the offset unit.

## 17.4.predict
Work out the whole table on paper.

## 17.4.size
The data is 2,600 minus 20, which is 2,580 bytes. Each fragment has room for 1,000 minus 20, or 980 bytes of data. But nonfinal payloads must be multiples of 8, so round down to 976.

## 17.4.slices
The payloads are 976, 976, and what's left, 628. Adding 20 byte headers gives total lengths of 996, 996, and 648. The offsets are 0, 122, and 244. The more fragments flags are 1, 1, and 0.

## 17.4.unit
Why 122? The offset field counts in units of 8 bytes of original data, and 976 divided by 8 is 122. It counts position in the data, not headers.

## 17.4.trap
The tempting answer cuts at 980, the space that's available. But then a second fragment would start at byte 980, and that position can't be written as a whole number of 8 byte units.

## 17.4.marks
On marks: one point each for the payload calculation, the totals, the offsets, the flags, and the explanation of the unit.
