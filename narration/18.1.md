# Dish 18.1: The packet story, from memory

## 18.1.open
It's Wednesday. Don't reread anything. Get a blank page, and draw the connected packet story from memory, one question at a time. I'll ask, you draw, and then I'll show you the answer. A laptop wakes up, and wants to reach a web server. Start at the very beginning. What does it need before it can send anything?

## 18.1.p1
Draw the host, the switch, the gateway, the DNS resolver, and the server. Then name what's missing.

## 18.1.a1
It needs an address, a mask, a gateway, and a resolver. DHCP supplies all four, in four messages: Discover, Offer, Request, ACK.

## 18.1.p2
The laptop wants the server's name resolved, and the resolver is on another network. What does it send first, and to whom?

## 18.1.a2
Its mask says the resolver is remote, so the first thing out is an ARP for the gateway. Then the DNS query leaves, with the gateway's MAC on the frame, and the resolver's address in the packet. The frame crosses one link. The packet crosses the network.

## 18.1.p3
The switch along the way had an empty table. What did it just learn, and what did it do with the frame?

## 18.1.a3
It learned the laptop's port from the source address. If the destination was unknown, it flooded. Once known, it forwards out one port. Source teaches. Destination decides.

## 18.1.p4
The packet reaches the gateway, headed for the server. What does the router look at, and what changes in the packet?

## 18.1.a4
It reads the destination IP, and picks the longest matching prefix. It replaces the Ethernet frame, and decrements TTL. The source and destination IP addresses stay put.

## 18.1.p5
Where did that forwarding table come from, inside one network, and between networks?

## 18.1.a5
Inside one AS, link state floods facts and runs Dijkstra, or distance vector trades distances with neighbors. Between ASes, BGP carries paths, shaped by policy and money. The chosen BGP next hop is resolved through internal routing.

## 18.1.p6
A link fails between the two routers. What goes wrong while the news spreads? Name two failure shapes.

## 18.1.a6
A blackhole, when a router holds an old route with no working continuation. And a transient loop, when routers install their changes at different times. Detect, disseminate, compute, install. Each stage takes time.

## 18.1.p7
Last one. The laptop leaves its wire and becomes a moving phone. What stays the same in the packet, and what changes?

## 18.1.a7
The inner packet stays the same, with its stable address. The outer destination changes, as a tunnel follows the phone. That's the picture from the very start, at a new scale: a local envelope, changing, around a global packet that isn't. If you could draw all of that, you have the story.
