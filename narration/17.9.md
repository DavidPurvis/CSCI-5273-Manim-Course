# Dish 17.9: Practice question 9: Dijkstra

## 17.9.question
Practice question 9. Undirected edges are A B 2, A C 6, B C 1, B D 5, and C D 2. Compute distances from A to B, C, and D. Give D's predecessor, and A's first hop toward D. State the ordinary edge-weight condition.

## 17.9.predict
Run it on paper. Keep distances, predecessors, and first hops separate.

## 17.9.run
Settle A. Tentative distances are B 2, C 6, and D infinity. Settle B at 2. Through B, C costs 3, which beats 6, and D costs 7. Settle C at 3. Through C, D costs 5, which beats 7. Settle D at 5.

## 17.9.answer
The distances are 2, 3, and 5. D's predecessor is C. But A's first hop toward D is B, since the path is A, B, C, D. And Dijkstra assumes nonnegative edge weights.

## 17.9.trap
The tempting mistake: D's predecessor is C, so A must send to C. No. The predecessor is the node just before D on the path. The first hop is the neighbor just after A.

## 17.9.marks
On marks: two points for final distances, one for the predecessor, one for the first hop, and one for the condition.
