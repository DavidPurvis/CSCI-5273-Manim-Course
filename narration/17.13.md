# Dish 17.13: Practice question 13: BGP selection

## 17.13.question
Practice question 13. Route A has local preference 150, AS length 5, and egress cost 20. B has preference 100, length 2, and cost 1. Which wins? If their local preference becomes equal, which wins? And how does the chosen BGP next hop become a usable local forwarding action?

## 17.13.predict
Choose both winners, and explain the next hop.

## 17.13.first
Local preference comes first. 150 beats 100, so A wins, and its longer path never matters.

## 17.13.second
Make the preferences equal. Now AS length decides. B's 2 beats A's 5, so B wins, before egress cost is ever compared.

## 17.13.resolve
The next hop. BGP's chosen next hop may be several routers away. It must resolve through internal reachability, to a usable output and an adjacent next hop. Only then can it be installed.

## 17.13.trap
The tempting answer adds the numbers into a score, or picks the lowest cost. BGP doesn't do that. It compares one attribute at a time, and stops as soon as one route remains.

## 17.13.marks
On marks: two points for the first choice and its reason, one for the second choice, and two for resolution.
