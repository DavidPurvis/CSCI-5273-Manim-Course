# Dish 17.11: Practice question 11: bad news

## 17.11.question
Practice question 11. X to Y costs 1, and Y to Z costs 1, with no other path to X. At first, Y reaches X directly, and Z uses Y. Then the link from X to Y fails. Show one circular stale-distance sequence. Explain exactly what Z advertises to Y under poison reverse, and give one limit of that technique.

## 17.11.predict
Write out the sequence, and the exact advertisement.

## 17.11.count
Z's old 2 leads Y to claim 3, through Z. Y's 3 leads Z to claim 4, through Y. Then Y can claim 5, and so on. No real path exists. The two routers are feeding each other.

## 17.11.poison
Under poison reverse, Z uses Y to reach X, so Z tells Y that its distance to X is infinity. That advertisement is specific to Y. Z's route is unchanged, and other neighbors can still hear the truth.

## 17.11.limit
The limit: it doesn't eliminate every longer dependency cycle. With three or more routers in a loop, the counting can still happen.

## 17.11.trap
The tempting answer says Z deletes its route, or tells everyone infinity. It doesn't. Z keeps its route, and only Y hears the infinity.

## 17.11.marks
On marks: two points for the circular trace, two for the exact poisoned direction and value, and one for a limitation.
