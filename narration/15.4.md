# Dish 15.4: Handover

## 15.4.question
A phone at highway speed leaves one cell, and enters the next, in the middle of a stream. The radio link must move. The tunnel must move. Packets are already in flight toward the old tower. How can you change all of this without dropping the session?

## 15.4.before
This is handover. Here's the setup. Packets come from the S-GW through a tunnel to base station A, then over the radio to the phone. Base station B sits next door, and it's stronger as the phone drives toward it. Base station A notices this from the phone's radio reports.

## 15.4.prepare
Step one. A chooses B as the target, and asks it to get ready. Step two. B reserves radio resources, and acknowledges. Nothing has moved yet, and the phone keeps working as before. Prepare first, and then act, so that the target is ready when the phone arrives.

## 15.4.switch
Step three. A tells the phone to switch, and the phone begins using B. The radio has moved. But the S-GW has not been told, and it's still sending to A. So what happens to those packets? Step four. A forwards them to B, over a temporary path. So during this period, the path is S-GW, A, B, phone. It's longer, but nothing's lost.

## 15.4.pathswitch
Step five. B reports that the move is complete. The MME tells the S-GW to change its tunnel endpoint from A to B. See that this is a control message, and the MME isn't carrying any data. Step six. The S-GW now sends straight to B, and A releases its old resources. The outer destination changed. The inner packet, and the phone's IP address, did not.

## 15.4.rule
There are three paths to remember. Before: S-GW, A, phone. During: S-GW, A, B, phone. After: S-GW, B, phone. The middle stage explains how the radio can finish moving before the core catches up.

## 15.4.transport
Does a preserved IP address mean nothing changes for TCP? No. The longer forwarding path raises delay for a while. Packets can arrive out of order. Radio errors and local retries make round trip times swing around. IP is still best effort, and its contract hasn't changed. But TCP might read these effects as congestion, even when no queue is full. The details of transport belong to later lectures. For now, keep the link: what lower layers do can change the performance higher layers see.

## 15.4.pitfall
During handover, notice who carries what. The MME only sends control messages. The data goes from the S-GW, through A, and on to B.

## 15.4.changed
Change the order. Imagine a design where the S-GW switches its tunnel to B first, before the phone has actually moved.

## 15.4.predict
What happens to packets for those moments? And is there any forwarding path that would save them?

## 15.4.answer
Packets reach B, but the phone is still attached to A, so B has nobody to give them to. They would need buffering, or they'd be lost. The real ordering avoids this: the radio moves first, and packets follow, by way of A, and only then does the core switch. Order matters, just as it did with routing convergence. And with that, every piece of the story is in place. So let's put it all together, one last time.
